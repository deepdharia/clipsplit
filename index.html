    Frequently asked questions

    Is ClipSplit really free?

    Yes — every feature on ClipSplit is completely free, with no accounts, no trial limits and no locked exports. The tool runs entirely in your browser, so there are no server costs to pass on to you.

    Does ClipSplit add a watermark?

    No. Clips exported from ClipSplit never have a watermark, on any mode or format. What you split is what you get — clean video ready to post.

    Are my videos uploaded to a server?

    No. ClipSplit processes your video 100% on your own device using your browser's built-in media engine. Your files are never uploaded, stored or seen by anyone else — you can even disconnect from the internet after the page loads and keep splitting.

    What video formats can I split?

    ClipSplit reads the formats most creators actually use: MP4, MOV, WebM and MKV, with the H.264, HEVC, VP9 and AV1 video codecs your browser can decode. Clips are always exported as widely-compatible MP4 files.

    Will splitting reduce my video quality?

    Not in the default Original mode. Clips are cut with stream copy, meaning the original video and audio data is passed through untouched — quality is bit-for-bit identical to the source and splitting takes seconds. Quality settings only apply when you convert a clip to 9:16 or 1:1, which requires re-encoding.

    Why do my cut points shift by a fraction of a second?

    To keep splitting instant and lossless, each cut snaps to the nearest keyframe at or just before your chosen point — usually within half a second. Cutting anywhere else would force a slow full re-encode of the whole video. ClipSplit always shows you the exact start time each clip actually got.

    Can I make vertical videos for Shorts, Reels and TikTok?

    Yes. Pick the 9:16 Crop mode for a full-frame vertical cut, or 9:16 Blur Background to keep the whole landscape frame with a stylish blurred fill — the same look top creators use. There's also a 1:1 Square mode for feed posts.

    Is there a file size limit?

    There is no server-side limit because there is no server — everything happens on your device. The practical limit is your device's memory: phones handle a few hundred megabytes comfortably, while desktops can chew through multi-gigabyte files. If a very large file struggles, try shorter clips or a desktop browser.
